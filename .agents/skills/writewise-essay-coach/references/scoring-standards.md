# Scoring Standards

These profiles are for practice feedback. They are not official score conversions.

## CET-4

- Scale: 15 points.
- Suggested length: 120-180 words.
- Internal criteria used by WriteWise: grammar 4, vocabulary 4, sentence variety 4, logic 3.
- Priority: basic accuracy, clear organization, understandable examples, and natural everyday academic language.
- Correction focus: spelling, subject-verb agreement, tense, verb form, sentence fragments, punctuation, common collocations.
- Polishing style: clear and controlled. Prefer a correct simple sentence over an inaccurate difficult sentence.

## CET-6

- Scale: 15 points.
- Suggested length: 150-200 words.
- Internal criteria used by WriteWise: grammar 4, vocabulary 4, sentence variety 4, argumentative coherence 3.
- Priority: accuracy plus lexical range, varied sentence patterns, and explicit links between ideas.
- Correction focus: all CET-4 fundamentals, plus word choice, collocation, repetition, paragraph cohesion, and awkward complex sentences.
- Polishing style: precise but natural. Improve lexical variety and sentence rhythm without making the essay sound inflated.

## Postgraduate English

- Default profile: English I large essay.
- Scale: 20 points.
- Suggested length for English I: 160-200 words. If the user specifies English II, use its shorter task requirements instead.
- Internal criteria used by WriteWise: grammar 5, vocabulary and collocation 5, sentence complexity 5, discourse logic 5.
- Priority: complete content points, visible argumentation, formal written English, paragraph organization, and controlled complexity.
- Correction focus: logic gaps, fragments, tense consistency, word choice, redundancy, weak topic sentences, and spoken-language phrasing.
- Polishing style: formal and analytical. Prefer a clear argument chain over decorative adjectives.

## IELTS Academic Task 1

- Scale: Band 1-9.
- Minimum suggested length: 150 words.
- Criteria and equal weight:
  1. Task Achievement
  2. Coherence and Cohesion
  3. Lexical Resource
  4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy
- Overall writing band: average the four criterion bands, then round to the nearest 0.5.
- Priority: write a clear overview, select and report key features, make accurate comparisons, and support descriptions with data.
- Common failure: treating a chart description as an opinion essay, listing every number without selecting key trends, or omitting an overview.
- Correction focus: objective reporting, data accuracy, comparison language, grouping, precision, complex-sentence accuracy, and spelling.
- Polishing style: academic but readable. Do not reward obscure vocabulary when it reduces precision or naturalness.

## IELTS Academic Task 2

- Scale: Band 1-9.
- Minimum suggested length: 250 words.
- Criteria and equal weight:
  1. Task Response
  2. Coherence and Cohesion
  3. Lexical Resource
  4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy
- Overall writing band: average the four criterion bands, then round to the nearest 0.5.
- Priority: answer every part of the question, maintain a clear position, develop ideas with examples, organize paragraphs, and use accurate varied grammar.
- Correction focus: task completeness, paragraph function, cohesion, collocation, precision, complex-sentence accuracy, and spelling.
- Polishing style: academic, precise, and readable. Do not reward obscure vocabulary when it reduces precision or naturalness.

## Same Idea, Different Target

Original:

> In my opinion, technology is very important for students.

- CET-4: `I believe technology is important for students.`
- CET-6: `From my perspective, technology plays an essential role in students' learning.`
- Postgraduate: `In my view, technology is crucial to students' academic development.`
- IELTS Task 2: `From my perspective, technology is of considerable importance to students' learning.`

These are style directions, not universal replacements. Preserve context and avoid repeating the same upgrade throughout an essay.

## Web App Acceptance Checks

After the user switches standards and explicitly activates “检查作文”, the resulting report should change all of the following:

- word-range guidance;
- score denominator and criterion maxima;
- criterion names for IELTS or postgraduate writing;
- priority and visible set of corrections;
- suggestions;
- polished wording.

Do not merely change the selected chip while keeping the previous report.

The correction list must hide punctuation-only complaints, collapse repeated instances of the same faulty phrase or rule, retain every unique grammar issue, and label contextually unnatural literal translations as 中式英语.

Mode changes only update the active rubric. They must not trigger a new analysis; wait for an explicit “检查作文” action.

Annotations belong directly inside the editable draft. Selecting a highlight shows its correction and reason and can replace the affected text in place. The 润色对比 view must use the first draft captured at the moment “检查作文” was activated, not later inline edits.

## Sentence and Structure Review

Review every sentence through its terminal punctuation before proposing a change. For each issue, identify the intended meaning and correct the full clause when a word-level substitution would remain unnatural.

Required structure feedback:

- quote or paraphrase the current opening and propose a stronger opening;
- assess paragraph order and identify paragraphs that mix multiple people, events, or arguments;
- show how a concrete event should progress from context to action to result;
- show how emotion can be expressed through detail instead of a direct label;
- quote or paraphrase the ending and propose a closing that resolves the article's central idea.

Annotated text colors:

- red: grammar, spelling, or word-choice errors;
- amber: 中式英语 or literal Chinese-to-English phrasing;
- blue: grammatical text that can be improved for the selected exam standard;
- split red/amber: one clause contains both a grammar issue and Chinese-influenced phrasing.

Use pale fills and darker text so annotations remain comfortable on light paper.

For the selected mode, include a concrete high-score blueprint based on the actual draft:

- CET-4/6 or postgraduate argument: opening and thesis, focused body paragraphs, conclusion that resolves the thesis;
- narrative: clear opening overview, chronological or person-by-person events, shown emotion, reflective ending;
- IELTS Task 1: topic paraphrase, overview, grouped features and comparisons;
- IELTS Task 2: paraphrase and position, developed body paragraphs, conclusion restating the position.
